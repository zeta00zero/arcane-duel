Arcane Duel Chronicles - How to play online
===========================================

1. Both players open the same game page (the same version of the game).
   Use the web link (itch.io / Netlify / GitHub Pages). It also works when the game is
   opened straight from the downloaded file (index.html) in a desktop browser
   (tested with Chrome/Chromium).
2. Player 1: Main menu > ONLINE DUEL > type your name > Create Room.
   You get a 6-character room code. Press "Copy code" (or "Copy invite link") and send
   it to your friend in any chat app.
3. Player 2: Main menu > ONLINE DUEL > type your name > enter the code > Join Room.
   (An invite link opens the Join screen with the code already filled in.)
4. Each player picks a duelist and a deck (Default, or the Custom deck saved in the
   Deck Builder). The room creator (the host) also picks the arena (home arena of either
   duelist, random, or a fixed arena) and an optional turn timer (90 or 120 seconds).
   Press Ready. The duel starts when both players are ready.

During the duel
- Your side is always at the bottom. Play exactly like single-player: drag & drop or click.
- When your opponent attacks, summons or plays a spell and you have a usable Set
  Quick-Play or Trap, you get the "Activate a Quick-Play?" prompt. You have 25 seconds;
  after that the game answers "Don't activate" for you. Your opponent sees "Waiting...".
- Quick chat buttons: Good luck!, Nice move!, Oops!, Well played!, Hmm..., GG.
- Surrender: the Surrender button under the quick chat. Leaving the duel also forfeits.
- After the duel: Rematch (both players press it), Change Duelist, or Leave Room.

Connection problems
- If a connection drops, the game reconnects by itself. If you closed or reloaded the
  page, open ONLINE DUEL again and join the same room code within 60 seconds. The duel
  continues where it was. After 60 seconds, the player who stayed wins by forfeit.
- The host (the player who created the room) must keep their page open. If the host's
  page is closed, the duel ends.
- Some networks (strict company or school networks, some mobile carriers) block direct
  connections. The game then tries a free public relay. If joining keeps failing, try
  another network (for example a phone hotspot), or enter your own TURN relay under
  ONLINE DUEL > Connection settings.

Privacy: no account and no sign-in. The free PeerJS server only sees the random room id.
Your duel data goes directly from browser to browser.
